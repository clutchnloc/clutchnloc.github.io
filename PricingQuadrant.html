Your Pricing Quadrant

Handle compliance first. If your state requires a license for the services you offer, resolve that before restructuring your pricing. Compliance is the floor of the business, and everything in this quadrant assumes you are standing on it.

Your price raise plan

At least one of your services is in Undercharging. Here is how to raise without losing the clients who built you:

  1. Give notice. Announce the change 30 to 60 days ahead, in writing, with your booking confirmations.
  2. Set one effective date. Every client hears the same date. No case-by-case negotiating at the chair.
  3. Decide on legacy rates. You do not have to charge loyal clients the max. A protected rate for the clients who built you keeps the clientele and environment you enjoy, while new clients come in at the new standard.
  4. Tie it to the standard. The price reflects what is delivered: your time standard, your consultation process, your consistency. Let the standard do the explaining.

Travel fee guidance

As a mobile loctician, keep your service price clean and charge the drive separately: a flat travel fee inside your normal radius (for example, 10 to 15 miles), then a per-mile rate beyond it. Folding travel into the service price hides your real rate and makes every quote a negotiation. The fee also filters clients who respect your time.

What these ranges assume

  • Standard maintenance window: clients within 4 to 7 weeks, not overdue (overdue starting prices are their own entries)
  • No repairs, slippage work, or severe buildup (those are add-on charges)
  • Install and extension prices are starting prices, adjusted up for length and density
  • Your standard rate, before travel fees, deposits, or overdue scaling
